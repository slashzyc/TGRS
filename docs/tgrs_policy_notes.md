## Format and review model

The [official TGRS author instructions](https://www.grss-ieee.org/publications/author-resources/tgrs-information-for-authors/) require PDF submissions, including revisions, in IEEE standard **double-column, single-spaced** format. Page numbers should run consecutively. The submission system adds line numbers, so manually adding them is unnecessary. References should use IEEE style. Figures and tables should be numbered, cited in the text, and placed near their discussion, with captions or titles that explain their content.

The [official TGRS peer-review page](https://www.grss-ieee.org/publications/author-resources/tgrs-peer-review/) describes **single-anonymous** review: reviewers know author identities. Adding the author list, affiliations, and corresponding-author information is therefore consistent with the journal's review model.

The [IEEE article-structure guidance](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/create-the-text-of-your-article/structure-your-article/) recommends a single-paragraph abstract of no more than 250 words. It should stand alone, and symbols or citations should be avoided. The final abstract must be checked against the completed manuscript rather than lengthened to satisfy a generic conference-writing prompt.

## Page length and 2026 charging threshold

For papers submitted after 1 January 2026, the TGRS author page states that mandatory overlength charges apply **beyond 10 printed pages, starting at page 11**. This is a charging threshold, **not a verified hard maximum manuscript length**. The current page charge and any membership or waiver rules should be checked again when the authors actually submit. No publication charge has been selected, incurred, or paid by this editing workflow.

The same page describes traditional and open-access routes. A publication route has not been selected on the authors' behalf.

## AI-assisted revision disclosure

The [IEEE journal submission and peer-review policy](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/) says that AI-generated content must be disclosed in the **Acknowledgments** section, identifying the system, the affected article sections, and the extent of use. Editing and grammar enhancement are generally outside the mandatory scope, but disclosure is recommended. The user explicitly requested a disclosure, and this revision includes one.

The policy also advises excluding references from ordinary AI grammar-editing passes. Here, reference changes are handled as a separate bibliographic check documented in [reference_verification.md](reference_verification.md), rather than unrestricted stylistic paraphrasing of titles or author names.

The disclosure must describe what occurred in **this revision**, without making unsupported claims about how the original study was conducted or written. A suitable form is:

> ChatGPT (OpenAI) was used during revision to assist with language editing, sentence-level rephrasing, and organization of the Abstract and the main text. No new experimental results were produced through this editing process.

The final version should name the actual affected sections or their final numbering. If table titles or figure captions were also revised, those changes should be included in the scope. The tool should not be listed as an author. An assertion that all authors have already reviewed, verified, or approved the AI-assisted changes must wait for actual human approval; this editing workflow does not record that event.

The purpose of the language revision is clearer and more natural scientific writing. No result from an AI-text detector was obtained, and no claim of detector avoidance or proof of exclusively human authorship is made.
